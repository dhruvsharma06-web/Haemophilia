# Exercise instructions and camera-rule references

Reviewed 13 October 2026. Primary sources are physiotherapy patient information from NHS hospitals and the World Federation of Hemophilia (WFH). These documents support the described movement; they do not validate this app or its camera scores. Different leaflets address different clinical populations.

## Angle convention

Clinical elbow/knee flexion usually starts at 0° when straight. The wireframe measures the **included angle**, which is approximately 180° when straight. Thus 45° of knee flexion corresponds to an included angle of 135°. A hip angle of 140° in the side-view straight-leg raise corresponds to approximately 40° elevation from the lying position. Shoulder elevation starts near 0° alongside the torso. Shoulder rotation uses a relative estimated forearm direction, not a clinical rotation measurement.

## What comes from engineering

Ready/target/return hold times (0.3/0.2/0.2 seconds), ±2° target margin, return bands, 0.3-second error confirmation, filtering, repetition onset, minimum ROM and score weights are engineering settings. The brief hold is an anti-jitter check, not the therapeutic hold duration in a leaflet. The component score is descriptive and is not a clinical judgment or a probability from a trained model.

The WFH guide recommends individual assessment and comparison with the person's available baseline. Its instructions are not grounds for forcing normal range in someone with joint limitation. Exact comfortable range and therapeutic hold duration remain the treating clinician's decision. A camera cannot verify muscle activation, pain, bleeding, bar grip, actual hand contact, weight distribution or all three-dimensional compensations. The app flags only the observable checks implemented in its rules.

## Verification scope

The regression suite checks the exact joint segments drawn by the six lower-body animations against the assessment engine, plus both elbow demonstrations. It also checks complete and incomplete cycles, supported versus unsupported assisted curls, bilateral returns, visible-side selection, inward/outward rotation with mirrored coordinates, transient versus sustained errors and green/red/neutral wireframe states. These are software correctness checks. They do not establish 100% accuracy for real users or replace phone movement checks.
